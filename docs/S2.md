# S2. Value and fractionality of the LP

The LP relaxation of the reference model B2 (561 variables, 116 rows, 51 variables fixed to 0)
was solved with scipy.optimize.linprog (method "highs", HiGHS, scipy 1.17.1) in about 0.01 s
(183 iterations). The optimal value is **z_LP = 8.339506** (cross-checks: HiGHS dual simplex and
interior point give the same value; PuLP 3.3.2 with CBC gives 8.339506 up to its printing precision).

In the optimal vertex **21 of the 499 free y_kl are fractional**, and **9 of the 50 VMs are split**
across several servers (VMs 2, 3, 10, 16, 20, 22, 26, 30, 43): 6 VMs are split over two servers and
3 VMs (2, 16, 20) over three. The other 41 VMs are placed integrally. The count depends on the
solver (the vertex is not unique in general); z_LP does not.

What the numbers say: the LP does not place VMs, it balances load. Because the latency
approximation is convex and increasing in the utilisation, the LP wants every server at the same
utilisation, and the fractions are what lets it reach that. Five servers (0, 1, 5, 8, 9) end at
u = 0.25 (t = 0.25), five (2, 3, 4, 6, 7) at u = 0.60 (t = 1.2222, exactly the kink of the
tangents at u = 0.4 and u = 0.7), and server 10 at u = 0.512. The utilisations are not equal
because memory (servers 3 and 7 use 100 % of their memory) and the zone restrictions of premium VMs
stop the balancing, and the splitting happens around exactly these servers (3, 7, 10, ...). So
the fractional entries are concentrated where capacity and zones bind. Whole VMs cannot be cut,
so every integer solution has to round these 9 VMs, and I expect z* to be noticeably above
8.339506 (to be checked in Checkpoint 4).
