# S3 — Is the solution optimal, and how do we know?

*Author: Daniyal Shaken (M3), team T10, storyline B2, seed 10.*

For the LP relaxation (561 variables, 116 rows) we get z_LP = 8.339506. For an LP the conditions (a)–(e)
(primal feasibility, signs of the dual prices, complementary slackness, reduced costs, strong duality) are
necessary and sufficient, so a pair (x, π) that satisfies all five to 1e-6 is a **certificate** of optimality, not
just evidence. The checker `check_kkt` in `src/kkt/` receives only (c, A, b, sense, lb, ub, x, π); it knows nothing
about the solver. For the HiGHS dual-simplex solution all five numbers are below 1e-14 (Table 2).

**Signs.** The most error-prone step is the sign of π. With scipy the marginals are ∂z/∂b of the rows as they were
passed, so a "≥" row stored as −A y ≤ −b returns −π and must be flipped. We tested this on two-variable LPs
with known prices (a "≥" row: +2; a "≤" row: −1). On the real instance flipping all signs is detected immediately:
(b) = 1.0, (d) = 2.0, (e) = 17; a perturbation of one x-entry by 1e-3 gives (a) = 1e-3.

**Other solvers.** HiGHS interior point returns another optimal vertex (22 fractional entries in 11 VMs instead of 21 in 9)
but the same z_LP and passes (a)–(e) too, which is what we expect: the vertex is not unique, z_LP is.
CBC (PuLP 3.3.2) passes (a)–(d) at about 3e-8, but (e) is 1.1e-6, just above the tolerance, because CBC
reports its duals with limited precision. We therefore treat 1e-6 on (e) as a solver-precision limit,
not as a modelling error. A tiny negative reduced cost on t_l (no upper bound) is tolerated in (e),
otherwise the dual objective would be −∞.

**What the prices say.** Only two families have nonzero prices: the placement rows (B1, Σπ = +14.38) and the
latency tangents (B4, Σπ = −11.00; 21 of 44 rows tight). CPU rows (B2) are never tight, and the memory rows (B3)
are tight in 2 cases with price 0 (primal degeneracy). No variable at its upper bound has a negative reduced cost,
so the term Σ min(0, r_j) in (e) is 0 and z_LP = bᵀπ. The cost is driven by the latency lines.

**Limits.** The certificate is for the LP only. It says nothing about an integer solution: 21 fractional entries
show that the vertex is not integral (Checkpoint 4).
